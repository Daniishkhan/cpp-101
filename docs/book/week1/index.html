Week 1 · Inference from games

C++ from zero to Life

Sixteen chapters that take you from "what is a program" to a Game of Life that you wrote yourself, tested, and timed, in one week.

What this book is

This is the Week 1 book of a 30-week curriculum (see goal.md) that ends with a quantised inference engine for a small Llama-architecture language model (SmolLM2-135M) in C++ and your own CUDA kernels. Week 1 looks nothing like that: it is a grid of # and . characters flickering in your terminal. Conway's Game of Life on a wrapping grid, with a configurable size and seed, a test suite that goes from red to green, and a first benchmark number.

It is written for one reader: you, a smart adult who is comfortable with a Mac and a terminal but has never written C++ and may never have programmed at all. Nothing is assumed. Every program is complete and was compiled and run on the machine described in docs/setup.md.

Budget: about five hours a day for seven days. Chapters 1 to 13 are reading with small experiments; chapter 14 is the challenge, which you hand-code one milestone at a time. The book teaches every mechanism on problems one step away from Life and never shows you the Life functions themselves. That part is yours.

How to read it

Read in order; each chapter uses only what came before it. Type every example into scratch/ and run it with tools/run.sh, rather than copying it. When a program's output is shown, compare it with yours character by character. Tick the box at the bottom of a chapter when you have worked through it; progress is saved in this browser.

Around the book

  • docs/setup.md: what is on your machine, what is missing, and a five-minute verification checklist. Do this before chapter 1.
  • The challenge README: the frozen spec for Game of Life, the six-function contract, the seven milestones and the acceptance tests.
  • goal.md: the whole 30-week plan and why Week 1 looks the way it does.
  • docs/tutor.md: the AI tutor in these pages (press A, or select any text). How to set it up with an API key, what it costs, and how it is kept from writing your Life for you.
  • CLAUDE.md: the rules your AI tutor follows here. It explains and reviews; it does not write your Life.

Serving the book locally

The book is plain HTML with no build step and no network access. You can open index.html directly in a browser, or serve the folder so that the address bar shows clean URLs:

$ python3 tools/serve-book.py
Serving the book at http://localhost:8765  (Ctrl-C to stop)

Then visit http://localhost:8765. The script is the built-in Python web server with one change: it tells the browser to re-check each page, so when the book is updated a normal reload shows the new version. Press Ctrl-C in that terminal to stop the server. Progress ticks are stored per browser and per origin, so stick to one way of opening the book if you want them to persist.

To check that every example in the book still compiles and prints what it claims:

$ python3 tools/book-check.py
